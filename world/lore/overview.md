# Kelston, briefly

Kelston grew where seven biomes meet: a river junction opening to the
coast, forest to one side, jungle to the other, mountains and tundra
behind, desert beyond the ridge. Every kind of citizen found the realm
that suited them - and then, being neighbors, they built one town anyway.

The founding insight, the one carved over the registry door, is that a
town that records things well only has to solve each problem once. The
standards office sits in the Commons between the assembly and the grand
market, and its work is treated like the waterworks: quiet, public,
essential. When the office revises a standard, the change ripples out the
same morning, and nobody's ledger breaks.

The deeper habit behind the standards is respect for biology. Kelston
never asks an owl to keep farmer's hours or a tortoise to keep a mouse's
calendar. It writes those differences down - size, hours, water, warmth,
lifespan - and builds every system to read them. The result feels less
like accommodation than like a town that simply fits.

Tone notes for contributors: warm, practical, lightly wondrous.
Infrastructure is celebrated the way other towns celebrate monuments.
Nothing is cringe-futuristic; the tech is the best of what we already
know how to do, plus a tiny bit further. Diet and predation stay out of
frame, always.
